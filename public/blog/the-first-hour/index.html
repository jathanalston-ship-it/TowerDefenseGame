RELEASE · November 4, 2026

The First Hour: fewer popups, more battlefields, and a game that tells you the truth

We played ASSIMILATION as a brand-new player and did not like everything we saw: eleven popups after the first run, every system unlocked inside an hour, one map, and a difficulty curve that went easy, easy, wall. This update fixes all of it — and adds thirteen battlefields, Rule Genes, the Threat ladder and real phone layouts on the way.

Every so often we wipe a profile and play ASSIMILATION the way a new player meets it: from the opening cinematic to the first loss. The last time we did, the game was deep and handsome and, in its first hour, exhausting. The first clear of Stage 1 ended in eleven "new system unlocked" cards, nine toasts underneath them, and an "Under-Powered" warning on the very next stage — right after a flawless win. Every one of thirty-four systems had revealed itself within about an hour. And all of it played out on one map.

This update is the answer. It is the largest change to the early game since launch, and most of it is about taking things away.

One reward, one summary, no lies

The tutorial now teaches the actual loop over two waves — wave, draft, build, wave — with callouts that measure the board and never sit on the lane, a draft card or your hero's ability. It ends with one graduation reward instead of two identical ones. After a run, three or more announcements fold into a single summary card; achievement toasts wait until it closes and collapse into one. Advice never points at a screen you can't open yet, and the readiness warning has a tolerance — 1,027 Power against a recommended 1,050 is a push, not an alarm. Lose a run and Results now tells you how close you were, what got through, and one thing to try.

We measure this now. A scripted first session runs against fixed budgets on every build: it counts the popups after the first run (10 before this update, 1 after), the time to your first tower (about 8 seconds once the cinematic is skipped), and the JavaScript a new player downloads before the game starts (down from 820 KB to about 518 KB, because hub screens now load on demand).

The hive opens up over hours, not minutes

New systems now reveal across your first ten-ish hours — Infinite after Stage 3, the Daily after Stage 5, Boss Rush after Stage 12, and the rest a wing or two at a time. Five side screens became tabs inside their hosts, the top bar shows a handful of currencies instead of ten, routine rewards claim themselves into the results of the run that earned them, and menu badges only appear where there is a decision to make. If you already had a screen, you keep it.

Thirteen battlefields

The arena used to be one map. It is now a registry of battlefields — twin lanes, an open maw, a maze-like coil, broken walls, flank funnels, a central breach — each one a row of data that a validator proves fair before it ships (every cell reaches the line, nothing blocks a build slot). The first three campaign stages use fixed maps chosen to teach; deeper stages roll a battlefield that suits their biome; the Daily rolls one for the world; Infinite shifts map with every biome. Walls are solid now, too: nothing gets shoved through rock.

While we were in there, we fixed the curve. Stages 1 and 2 were trivially easy and Stage 3 was a wall; a gentler grace window, scaled-down first bosses, and invasions limited to each stage's own enemies now make the first three stages rise steadily.

Rule Genes, Hive Intelligence and the Threat ladder

Most gene cards bend numbers. Twenty-five new Rule Genes bend rules — shred builds, spreading infections, conveyor economies, placement tricks, hero stances, and deliberate risk-for-reward bargains — and once you have earned one, every draft guarantees a rule-changing option. Hive Intelligence, the one-button Biomass planner, now works only on content you have already beaten: it is the reward for mastery, not a replacement for it. And every mode you have cleared opens the Threat ladder — ten named rungs of difficulty that pay titles, profile frames and a little more reward, and never Power.

A board-first screen, on every screen

The run layout was rebuilt around the largest board the window allows — a third bigger at 1440×900 — with speed, Hive Intelligence and Pause sharing one strip so nothing overlaps the Start Wave button again. Phones get real portrait and landscape layouts with drawers and thumb-sized targets, and a phone held upright no longer asks you to rotate during a run. And yes, the hero health bar that briefly read 359/248 is fixed.

A note on the store

In Belgium and the Netherlands, loot-box law treats a randomized reward bought with money — or with a currency bought with money — as gambling. Rather than argue the edge cases, we took the strict reading: in those countries we don't sell anything that can end in a random reward. The whole game, containers included, stays earnable in play. Everywhere else, container drop rates are shown in the Vault.

What's next

More battlefields and Threat rungs are now data rows, so expect them often. New tower strains and heroes are next on the roadmap, and the first-session probe means every future update gets measured against a new player's first hour before it ships. Patch notes for this update are on the News page.
